EU envoy warns Bangladesh needs fairer trade terms before LDC graduation

Staff Correspondent 7 মে 2026 মূল ইংরেজি পড়ুন ↗

Bangladesh is approaching a critical economic turning point as it prepares for graduation from the Least Developed Country (LDC) category, with the European Union warning that the country will need more balanced trade arrangements to sustain its growth after the transition.

EU Ambassador to Bangladesh Michael Miller said Bangladesh now faces a “key graduation milestone” and stressed the importance of building stronger and more equal trade ties with the European bloc ahead of the scheduled graduation process.

His remarks came amid increasing concern over the future of Bangladesh’s export competitiveness once preferential trade benefits begin to phase out.

The European Union remains Bangladesh’s largest export destination, particularly for the ready-made garment sector, which heavily depends on duty-free market access under existing trade privileges.

Economists and trade experts fear that losing those benefits after graduation could significantly raise export costs and weaken Bangladesh’s competitive edge in global markets.

Ambassador Miller emphasised that future cooperation should move beyond traditional aid-based relations and evolve into a more balanced economic partnership focused on trade, investment and sustainable development.

The EU has recently intensified diplomatic engagement with Dhaka, including discussions on preferential market access, investment protection and broader economic cooperation.

Bangladesh has already sought additional time for LDC graduation, arguing that the economy remains vulnerable due to global instability, inflationary pressure and domestic financial challenges.

Analysts say the transition will require major reforms in trade policy, banking, taxation and industrial diversification to reduce dependence on apparel exports.

Trade observers believe the coming negotiations with the EU could prove decisive for Bangladesh’s post-LDC future, especially as Dhaka seeks continued market access under the EU’s GSP+ framework while protecting its export-driven industries from sudden tariff shocks.

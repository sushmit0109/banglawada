Fire at hospital nursery in Pakistan's capital kills 14 newborns

At least one baby was saved by a doctor, hospital spokesperson says

Waadaa Desk 26 আগস্ট 2026 মূল ইংরেজি পড়ুন ↗

A blaze erupted around 6:45am in the Mother and Child Hospital ward at Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences in Islamabad, apparently triggered by an air-conditioning unit failure, killing 14 newborns. Officials said 15 babies were in the ward and at least one was rescued. The incident, among Pakistan’s deadliest hospital fires, has prompted an official probe and renewed scrutiny of under-resourced public healthcare.

A fire broke out early Wednesday at a newborn nursery in a major government hospital in Pakistan's capital, killing 14 infants, hospital officials and the government said.

Rescue officials said the fire started around 6:45am, apparently after the compressor of an air-conditioning unit exploded inside the nursery on the third floor of the state-run Pakistan Institute of ​Medical Sciences (PIMS).

এক বিবৃতিতে ইসলামাবাদ প্রশাসন জানিয়েছে, মা ও শিশু হাসপাতালের ওয়ার্ডে আগুন লেগেছে।

এতে বলা হয়, উদ্ধারকারী দল ঘটনাস্থলে পৌঁছেছে, ছয়টি ফায়ার ব্রিগেডের গাড়ি এবং চারটি অ্যাম্বুলেন্স উদ্ধার অভিযানে অংশ নিয়েছে।

"আগুন অবিলম্বে নিয়ন্ত্রণে আনা হয়েছে এবং শিশুদের অবিলম্বে নিবিড় পরিচর্যা ইউনিটে স্থানান্তরিত করা হয়েছে," এতে বলা হয়েছে, 14 শিশু "সম্পূর্ণভাবে দগ্ধ হয়েছে"।

An inquiry committee headed by the additional deputy commissioner general has been formed to investigate the incident, it added.

Meanwhile, PIMS Spokesperson Dr Aneeza Jalil toldDawnthat 15 babies were in the ward.

“At least one baby was saved by a doctor,” she said.

Other media reports, however, put the number of babies at 16, with some suggesting the death toll to be higher at 15.

কথা বলছেনজিও নিউজ, Health Minister Mustafa Kamal said the infants in the ward were either in incubators or on oxygen.

The incident is among the deadliest hospital fires ​in recent years in the country, where government healthcare is subsidised but facilities are under-resourced and overburdened while watchdogs have ​frequently criticised state-run facilities as mismanaged.

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has directed authorities to conduct an immediate investigation, his office said.

"Those responsible should be identified, and the ​strictest action should ​be taken," ⁠Sharif's office said in a statement, adding, "A tragedy involving children is an irreparable loss."

The ward has been cordoned off and ambulances were ​seen outside the hospital building, according ​to ⁠a Reuters witness.

PIMS, where former Prime Minister Imran Khan was briefly treated last week, is ⁠one ​of the main public hospitals ​of the city.

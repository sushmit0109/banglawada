Budget 2026–27: Products that may get cheaper

Staff Correspondent 11 জুন 2026 মূল ইংরেজি পড়ুন ↗

Finance Minister Amir Khasru Mahmud Chowdhury has announced the proposed budget for fiscal year 2026–27. The budget proposes reducing or withdrawing taxes and duties on more than 100 goods and services to promote public health, agriculture, information technology, environmentally friendly transportation, industrialisation, and the digital economy. As a result, the production costs of many goods may decline, and the market prices of some products may also decrease.

Essential goods

The government has taken a major people-oriented step to reduce the cost of living and curb inflation. The proposed budget seeks to significantly reduce the tax deducted at source on 60 essential agricultural products, including rice, pulses, edible oil, and onions.

This initiative aims to provide relief to consumers. Previously, the tax-at-source rates on various daily necessities and agricultural products ranged from 1% to 5%, depending on the product. The new proposal seeks to reduce all such taxes to just 0.5%.

Dates

To provide relief to consumers throughout the year, including during the holy month of Ramadan, the budget proposes withdrawing the existing 5% regulatory duty on date imports. As a result, import costs may decline, leading to slightly lower retail prices.

Spices

The budget proposes withdrawing the 5% regulatory duty on the import of all types of spices. This may positively affect the prices of import-dependent spices such as cinnamon, cardamom, cloves, and pepper.

Baby food

The proposal seeks to reduce the import duty on raw materials used in baby food production from 15% to 10%. This may lower production costs for locally manufactured baby food, making it more affordable and accessible to consumers.

Fertiliser

The budget proposes withdrawing the 7.5% VAT imposed at the fertiliser business level. It also proposes reducing the import duty on zinc ash, a key raw material used in zinc sulphate fertiliser production, to zero.

Pesticides

The proposal seeks to completely withdraw VAT on the import of raw materials used in pesticide production and waive the 7.5% advance tax on imported pesticides. This may help reduce farming costs.

Poultry, dairy and fish feed

The budget proposes granting zero-duty benefits on three key raw materials used in the production of poultry, dairy, and fish feed. This may reduce feed production costs.

Poultry equipment

The proposal seeks to make the import of machinery and spare parts for the poultry and dairy sectors duty-free, allowing farmers to purchase equipment at lower prices.

Kidney dialysis filters

Dialysis filters are essential medical devices used to treat patients suffering from kidney disease. The budget proposes withdrawing the 15% VAT and 5% advance income tax on kidney dialysis filters.

According to government estimates, this could reduce the cost of each dialysis session by approximately Tk 800.

Blood tubing sets

The proposal seeks to withdraw the 7.5% advance tax on the import of blood tubing sets used in haemodialysis.

Heart rings (Stents)

The budget proposes withdrawing the 10% VAT on the supply of heart rings, or stents. This could reduce the price of each stent by around Tk 20,000.

Intraocular lenses

The withdrawal of VAT on intraocular lenses could reduce the price of each lens by approximately Tk 5,000.

Cost of preserving  bodies

The proposal seeks to reduce the import duty on mortuary equipment used for preserving bodies from 25% to 1%.

Cancer drugs

The budget proposes reducing the duty and VAT on nine new raw materials used in the production of anti-cancer drugs to zero.

Medicines

The proposal seeks to completely withdraw import duties on 51 new materials used in the production of active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs). This may help lower medicine prices in the domestic market.

Raw materials for the pharmaceutical industry

To sustain the growth of Bangladesh's pharmaceutical exports, the budget proposes including 17 additional basic raw materials under existing concessional benefits and reducing their import duty to zero.

Laptops, desktop computers and printers

To support the development of the IT sector and create employment opportunities, the budget proposes withdrawing all import duties, regulatory duties, supplementary duties, and VAT on laptops, desktop computers, servers, computer printers, and computer monitors.

Computer SSDs

The proposal seeks to withdraw all regulatory duties, supplementary duties, and VAT on SSD imports, while retaining only the 5% import duty.

Servers

Duty and tax exemptions are being provided on server imports.

POS machines

The budget proposes reducing the import duty on point-of-sale (POS) machines from 10% to 5% and eliminating the advance tax. This is expected to have a positive impact on the market.

Electric vehicles

The proposal seeks to reduce the tax burden on electric vehicles valued at up to $25,000 from 93% to 64%, and on vehicles valued at up to $50,000 to 80%. This may lower EV prices.

Plug-in hybrid behicles

The budget proposes reducing the total tax burden on plug-in hybrid vehicles with engine capacities of up to 1,800cc from 93.16% to 73.43%.

EV chargers and charging stations

The proposal seeks to completely withdraw the existing 39.75% tax on electric vehicle chargers and charging stations. This may help make charging services more affordable.

Musical instruments

The budget proposes withdrawing the 5% regulatory duty on the import of musical instruments and parts, including guitars, pianos, and violins.

Cinema cameras

The proposed budget seeks to reduce the import duty on high-tech cinematographic cameras from 15% to 5%.

Assistive devices for people with special needs

A complete exemption from all import duties, regulatory duties, supplementary duties, and advance taxes has been proposed for the import of 21 types of assistive devices designed to support independent mobility for people with special needs.

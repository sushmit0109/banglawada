Kanchan Guava: From Chattogram's hills to the Gulf, a Tk6 crore journey

A famed local guava carves a space in Middle Eastern shelves as it battles perishability and weak supply chain infrastructure

BSS 26 আগস্ট 2026 মূল ইংরেজি পড়ুন ↗

From its origins in Kanchannagar “Guava Village,” Kanchan Guava now reaches shelves in Dhaka and Gulf states like Saudi Arabia and the UAE, generating Tk1.5–2 crore in exports annually. Farmers can earn up to Tk150,000 per acre, but inadequate cold-chain infrastructure, modern packaging and storage facilities prevent them from fully capitalising on rising global demand.

The reputation of the traditional 'Kanchan Guava' has transcended national boundaries, with the fruit continuing to find a place on grocery shelves across the Middle East, from the hilly terrain of Chandanaish and Patiya upazilas in Chattogram.

Its unmatched flavour, appealing colour, distinctive shape and prolonged freshness make it a favourite among guava lovers.

কৃষকরা প্রায় উচ্চ আয়ের নিশ্চয়তা দিতে পারে, যদিও এখনও অনেক কিছু করা যেতে পারে।

Sagir Ahmed, a farmer, noted that with diligent effort and proper garden maintenance, one can earn up to 150,000 taka per acre during the season, which can often mean almost double the initial investment.

The guava variety has already carved out a six-crore-taka market for itself, considering both domestic and export sales.

But Sagir says that due to the perishable nature of guava, farmers are often unable to secure a reasonable price.

"বাগান থেকে ফসল তোলার পর, অবিলম্বে পেয়ারা বিক্রি করা অপরিহার্য। এই অঞ্চলে পেয়ারা সংরক্ষণের জন্য হিমাগার স্থাপন অপরিহার্য," তিনি যোগ করেন।

চন্দনাইশ ও পটিয়া উপজেলায় ৮৩০ একর জমিতে কাঞ্চন পেয়ারার চাষ হয়। চলতি বছরের আগস্টের শুরুতে এটি বাজারে আসে।

আগস্টের শুরু থেকে পেয়ারা চট্টগ্রামের পাশাপাশি ঢাকা, কুমিল্লা, ফেনী, নোয়াখালী, সিলেট, খুলনা ও রাজশাহীর বিভিন্ন স্থানে বিক্রি হচ্ছে।

এছাড়া প্রতি বছর প্রায় দেড় থেকে দুই কোটি টাকার ফল সৌদি আরব, সংযুক্ত আরব আমিরাত, ওমান, কাতার ও কুয়েতের মতো গন্তব্যে রপ্তানি করা হয়।

এমনকি অব্যবহৃত সম্ভাবনা থেকে যায়।

Mohammad Ismail Chowdhury Hanif, proprietor of Chattogram Foods and Vegetables, a fruit and vegetable exporting company, said that although demand for Kanchan guava in foreign markets has increased, there are several structural limitations in the export process.

He said it is important to modernise the entire system, from production to marketing, to fully establish guava as an exportable agricultural product.

To this end, he said emphasis has to be placed mainly on two aspects — an international-standard packaging system and storage facilities and cold-chain infrastructure.

“Without these, exports will not increase as per the potential. If an international-standard 'packing house' is built at Chattogram Shah Amanat International Airport for the export of quality vegetables and fruits, it is possible to increase the amount of exports many times,” he said.

Rabiul Hossain, a guava farmer from Kharna village in Patiya, echoes the sentiment.

“This fruit is in high demand in remote areas. Although discussions have taken place regarding the construction of cold storage by the upazila administration, no tangible progress has been made yet,” he said.

Regarding fair prices, Chandanaish Upazila Agriculture Officer Md Azad Hossain said, “If jam and jelly producers establish long-term contracts with guava farmers, the latter will receive a fair price.”

A local legend transforming fortunes

Runa Saha, Assistant Professor of the Department of Economics at the University of Chattogram, said, “If the export of agricultural products increases, it will create opportunities to earn foreign exchange.

“On the other hand, it will increase jobs and farmers' income at the local level.”

Farmer Jane Alam from Chandanaish said, “I have cultivated guava on approximately 8 acres of land. Ten workers are employed in the garden, earning between 600-800 taka each.”

“A basket contains around 250 guavas. At the start of the current season, a pair of guava baskets was sold for 2,500 taka. However, the price tends to decline as the season progresses,” he added.

In addition to Kanchannagar in Chandanaish, the guava is also cultivated in approximately 2,000 gardens spanning 750 hectares across various hilly regions, including Dhopachhari, Hashimpur, Jungle Hashimpur, Allahabad, Dohazari and Jungle Jamizuri in the upazila.

Besides, around 80 hectares of land in the hilly areas of Shrimai, Kharna and Haidgaon in Patiya upazila are also dedicated to guava cultivation.

As guava is grown without the use of pesticides, the produce can be classified as organic guava.

Local guava farmers assert that the fruit is nutrient-rich and possesses a distinctive flavour. It is sweet, contains very few hard seeds and is visually appealing.

When ripe, the flesh can be white, yellow or reddish.

The term 'Kanchan Guava' originates from the fact that this particular variety was first cultivated in Kanchannagar village of Chandanaish upazila in Chattogram.

This guava has bestowed a unique identity upon the village, which is now recognised as 'Guava Village', and the fruit has achieved renown as 'Kanchan Guava.'

The cultivation of guava has since expanded to the adjacent upazila of Patiya and the surrounding hilly regions.

Local school teacher Subhash Mallick noted that during the guava season, temporary markets for guava are set up at various locations within the upazila, including Kanchannagar in Chandanaish, Kanchannagar Railway Station, in front of BGC Trust University, Khanhat, Gachbaria College Gate, Badamtal, Rawshan Hat, Bagicha Hat, Dohazari, Kamal Munshir Hat and Kharna bus stand in Patiya. Wholesale traders frequently visit these markets.
